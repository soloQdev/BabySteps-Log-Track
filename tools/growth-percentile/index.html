How this is calculated

The WHO Child Growth Standards give reference curves for the 3rd, 15th, 50th, 85th and 97th percentiles at each month from birth to 24 months. This calculator finds those curves at your baby's exact age, then estimates where the measurement falls between them.

Because it works from five curves rather than the full WHO tables, the result is an estimate. It is shown as “about” and rounded to a whole percentile, and it is not given outside the range from the 3rd to the 97th percentile.

What a percentile means

A percentile compares one measurement with a large group of healthy babies of the same age and sex. About the 30th percentile means that about 30 in 100 babies had a lower measurement and about 70 had a higher one. It is a comparison, not a score: babies of every size grow well.

One measurement says less than the pattern over several visits. Your health professional looks at that pattern, at how your baby is feeding and at how they are doing overall.

Source: World Health Organization, WHO Child Growth Standards (weight-for-age, length-for-age and head circumference-for-age), 2006.
